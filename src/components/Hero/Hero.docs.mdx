# Hero

A full-viewport-height image at the top of a page, optionally with content (usually a `PageHeader`) centred over it.

<Primary />

## When to use

- At the top of a page that opens with a strong image, such as a photography post or a landing page.

## When not to use

- **Anywhere but the top of a page:** use `FeaturedSection` with an image background.
- **For pages without a strong image:** use a plain `PageHeader`.
- **More than once per page.**

## Variants

### Image only

With no children, the image shows with no overlay.

<Canvas of={HeroStories.Default} />

### With a heading

Put a `PageHeader` inside. An overlay is added behind it.

<Canvas of={HeroStories.WithHeader} />

## Sizing

- **Height:** `--hero-height` (default `100vh`).
- **Crop:** `--themed-component-hero-image-fit` (default `cover`).
- **Space below:** `--themed-component-hero-margin-bottom` (8rem, or 4rem at 568px and below).

See [Theming](?path=/docs/theming--docs).

## Accessibility

**Your responsibility:**

- **One `h1`:** a `PageHeader` inside the Hero is the page's `h1`. Don't add another `PageHeader` below it.
- **Text over the image:** the overlay is light (10% in light mode, 30% in dark mode). It doesn't guarantee contrast. Choose an image with a calm area behind the heading, and check contrast at every screen size, since `cover` crops differently on narrow screens.
- **Image alt text:** if the image is mainly mood, give it `alt=""`. If it shows something the page is about, describe it.
- **Height:** 100vh pushes all content below the fold. Consider a shorter `--hero-height` on content-heavy pages.

## Do and don't

- **Do** pair Hero with a short `PageHeader`.
- **Don't** put long text or several actions over the image.
